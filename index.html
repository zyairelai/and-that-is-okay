A chronicle of quiet devotion

Chapter I

6th June 2026

He had liked her since 2023,

A ghost hidden behind the screen,

Never daring to step into her light.

In 2025, he finally took the shot.

The date felt like a beautiful dream,

And her words were honey:

“I really like you, I just need more time.”

He believed it.

She ghosted him for months, deactivating her world back and forth,

Leaving him stranded in the misery.

Meanwhile, he was surviving.

Trying so hard to work, carrying her name as his only fuel.

Her birthdate memorized, the dream vacation promised in his heart.

She was always “busy”, understandable.

She was busy with her own life.

And that is okay.

Eight months of active chasing,

Answered only by the cold mechanics of a push-and-pull.

Her IG followers count ticked up from 48 to 88,

Her vlogs were filmed, edited, and shared with the world.

Yet his messages remained stranded at a single tick.

Sent, but never worthy of her energy.

Every ounce of his effort fell into a void, unvalidated.

Maybe she is not busy.

Maybe he is just a low priority.

And that is okay.

She said she was busy.

She said she wasn't ready.

She said she was uncomfortable.

Yet, her time and capacity were never truly full.

They were simply reserved for anyone but him.

Maybe it was never about her inability to give,

But his inability to realize he was never a priority.

And that is okay.

Maybe the problem was never about the IG numbers on a screen.

Maybe the tragedy is simply a one-sided emotional investment,

Clinging to an unavailable heart.

And that is okay.

Maybe she could have been merciful enough to say:

“Stop. It doesn’t work out.”

But she chose the cruel comfort of fake hope:

“I really like you, I am just not comfortable yet.”

And that is okay.

Chapter II

7th November 2026

How harsh can one person be,

To delete a full year in a single second?

A shared history wiped from both screens,

As if devotion was just a mistake to reckon.

His effort, his sincerity, his genuine heart,

Erased by a click, pulled completely apart.

Leaving him asking if he ever mattered at all,

Or if he was just waiting for an empty fall.

And that is okay.

Twelve months.

If 12 months of effort cannot bring comfort,

Maybe that was the answer all along.

Maybe he was never her kitten,

Just a disturbing factor in her perfect life.

And that is okay.

It has to be okay.
